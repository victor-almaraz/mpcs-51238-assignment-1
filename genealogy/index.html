A folio from the bookshelf

The genealogy of the rooms

How each room was made from the ones before it: a plain page, three ways of laying it out, eleven styles on the one with tabs, then the styles crossed with each other into desks, desktops, a game, a magazine and the room. A full line runs from a room to one it builds on; a dashed line to one it took a single part from. Point at a room to see its line.

The lineage

  1. No. 01The working coreThe first room. The interpreter and the sieve solver made to work, in black text on white with the least styling.
  2. No. 02Sub-pagesFrom No. 01. The one long scroll split into sub-pages for the instructions, the editor, the card viewer and the output.
  3. No. 03TabsFrom No. 01. The same four parts behind tabs: the layout every themed version starts from.
  4. No. 04Thermal paper and typewriterFrom No. 03. Printouts on thermal paper and typewritten documents: monochrome, serif, yellowed, with scanner dust.
  5. No. 05MicroficheFrom No. 03. High contrast, monochromatic, grainy and ragged, as a frame of microfiche on a reader.
  6. No. 06ScrapbookFrom No. 03. An eclectic scrapbook of collected paper artifacts, and the first of the miscellanea.
  7. No. 07Cray-2From No. 03. The bold colours and geometric shapes of the Cray-2 supercomputer.
  8. No. 08Ordered ditheringFrom No. 03. Vintage computer graphics: ordered dithering with at most eight steps to a channel.
  9. No. 09Swiss style in hatchingFrom No. 03. The Swiss and International styles, with hatching in place of colour.
  10. No. 10HalftoneFrom No. 03. Everything printed through a halftone screen.
  11. No. 11DadaFrom No. 03. The page as a Dada collage, with experimental decks of chance.
  12. No. 12The BackroomsFrom No. 03. The look and feel of the Backrooms: the page as a liminal space, an echo of the past.
  13. No. 13German ExpressionismFrom No. 03. The look and cinematography of German Expressionist film.
  14. No. 14Reflection in a Dead DiamondFrom No. 03. The cinematography and set design of Cattet and Forzani’s film: bold, slightly odd 1960s design.
  15. No. 15A physical workspaceFrom No. 04 and No. 06. A skeuomorph of a desk, its materials in books, notebooks and decks, with music decks and a reel-to-reel player.
  16. No. 16A digital workspaceFrom No. 08, with a part of No. 15. A vintage computer desktop with a buffer editor and a player program, and v15’s miscellanea as documents.
  17. No. 17A game of punch cardsFrom No. 09. v9’s style for a puzzle game that teaches how to write and use punch cards.
  18. No. 18Code art in halftoneFrom No. 10 and No. 14, with a part of No. 11 and No. 12. v14’s graphics through v10’s halftone, and the experimental decks of v11 and v12, framed by code art.
  19. No. 19The workspace with a magazineFrom No. 15 and No. 18. v15’s desk tightened, with v18 as a magazine on it whose decks can be run.
  20. No. 20Puzzles on the desktopFrom No. 16 and No. 17. v17’s puzzles inside v16’s desktop, its graphics refined in v17’s design language.
  21. No. 21A desk with a computerFrom No. 19 and No. 20. One workspace: v19’s desk with a computer on it that runs v20’s desktop, each half in its own style.
  22. No. 22VectorFrom No. 21. v21 in one style: the computer redrawn as a modern vector interface, and things in the room that can be changed.
  23. No. 23Pixel artFrom No. 21. v21 in one style: the room as pixel art, the computer in full-colour pixels.
  24. No. 24Pixel art with variationsFrom No. 23 and No. 22. v23’s pixel room with v22’s variations, lit for every lamp and every time of day.
  25. No. 25The room, perfected and expanded (in progress)From No. 24. v24 perfected and expanded into a space to wander: a reading corner round the corner, machines under the desk, and nooks to get lost in.
